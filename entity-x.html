The short answer

Entity X is the largest single Kaspa address. The name is just a label that block explorers like kaspa.stream gave it — nobody knows who controls it. What the on-chain record shows

  • It buys every single week. In the week ending July 27, 2026 it added ~17M KAS, withdrawn directly from Bitget and Gate.io. In the 90 days before that: ~84M KAS.
  • Coins have left it — 39,038,233 KAS in 20 transfers since March 2024. You will read almost everywhere that this wallet has never sent a coin out. That is simply not true, and we only know because we rebuilt the address from every transaction ourselves. It is still a small number: 2.54% of everything the wallet ever bought, against 97.46% still sitting there. Every single transfer is listed below.
  • It reached this size fast. Between April and July 2026 alone the balance grew from about 1.41 billion to just under 1.5 billion KAS — and it kept going.
  • First activity: March 2024. A few hundred transactions total — large, deliberate withdrawals, not trading.

What Entity X actually paid

Everyone quotes this wallet's balance. Almost nobody has worked out what it cost. So we did it ourselves, from the raw chain data, and we publish the method along with the number.

We pull every transaction this address has ever been part of, keep only the true net inflows, and value each one at the volume weighted average price of the day it landed — not the daily close. Nobody buys at midnight. That single choice is why our number will differ slightly from anyone using closing prices, and it is the more honest of the two.

Who is behind it?

Honestly: nobody knows, and anyone who claims to is guessing. The plausible theories are a very early large holder consolidating, an institution or fund custodying a position, or an OTC desk's cold storage. What the chain rules out is just as useful: the pattern — steady weekly withdrawals from exchanges, buying roughly forty times faster than it sends anything out — looks like patient accumulation, not market-making or exchange infrastructure.

Why it matters

Two reasons. First, concentration: one unknown party holding 5.4% of supply is a real risk factor — if this wallet ever moved toward an exchange, it would matter, fast. Second, signal: someone with tens of millions of dollars keeps choosing to take KAS off exchanges week after week — straight through the 2025–2026 drawdown. You don't have to copy them. But it's one of the few on-chain facts in crypto you can verify yourself in 30 seconds.
