what this format is

A weekly chart breakdown for Kaspa that never tells you where the price is going. We show where the close sits against two mechanical lines, what one KAS buys in bitcoin, and the price bands where trading actually happened. Then one sentence on what would change our read. Published every Monday at 16:00 CET, the same hour as the dashboard and the newsletter.

The two lines

Both lines are mechanical. Nothing is drawn by hand, because a line dragged with a cursor cannot be checked by anyone.

The 21 week average

The average of the last 21 weekly closes, weighted so recent weeks count more (an exponential average, seeded with the plain average of the first 21 weeks). It reacts within a few weeks, so it answers a short question: is this week trading above or below where the last few months traded? Right now the close sits — it, and Kaspa has closed below this line — weeks in a row. The last weekly close above it was —.

The 50 week average

The plain average of the last 50 weekly closes. Longer memory, slower to turn, so it answers the year-shaped question. The close sits — it. In our data window, Kaspa has closed a week above that line —.

Kaspa priced in bitcoin

The chart almost nobody shows, because the answer is often uncomfortable. A satoshi is the smallest piece of a bitcoin, one hundred millionth. Pricing KAS in sats answers the only question a bitcoin holder actually has: was holding Kaspa instead of bitcoin worth it?

Both charts can be true in the same week. The dollar chart can have a strong week while the bitcoin chart keeps falling, because the dollar chart measures the whole market and the bitcoin chart measures the race inside it.

The shelf, where trading actually happened

Our honest version of support and resistance. Instead of drawing a line where a chart looks pretty, we count. Every week of trading goes into a price band half a cent wide, and each band gets credited with that week's volume. The bands with the most volume are the prices where the most coins actually changed hands, so those are the prices where the most holders have something at stake.

A band is not a floor. Heavy volume tells you where positions were built, not where the price has to stop. We report the count, never a prediction from it.

How we count

The data window. Our candle file starts on —, the first day of the exchange feed we log ourselves. It is not the start of Kaspa trading. Every "since" and every "in our window" on this page and in the videos means that window, and we say so rather than implying we have the full history.

The source. Kraken daily candles, UTC close, logged by us into a file in the open. A week ends Sunday 00:00 UTC, which makes Saturday's reading the weekly close. Bitcoin comes from the same log, so the sats number is one file divided by another, not a figure copied from a third party.

What we refuse to do. No price targets, no time frames, no "could" and no "should", in the video, in the comments and in replies. Every line is either mechanical or counted by us. If someone asks for a target, the answer is always the same one: we do not do targets, the levels are on this page, the chain numbers are counted, the call is yours.
