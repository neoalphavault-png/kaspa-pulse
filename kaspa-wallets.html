Which Kaspa wallet is safe? The list with zero affiliate links on it.

Search "best Kaspa wallet" and every list on page one earns a commission from its own ranking. This page earns nothing from your choice. Here is what the Kaspa community actually maintains, the two deprecated wallets still being recommended elsewhere, why your address keeps changing, and the five rules that keep your coins yours.
The short answer

On mobile, Kaspium. On desktop, Kaspa NG. In cold storage, Tangem natively or a Ledger via KasVault. For KRC-20 tokens in the browser, Kasware. All of them are listed by the community's own directory, and all except the hardware devices are free.

Just as important: two wallets you will still find recommended in old guides are deprecated — KDX and the old web wallet at wallet.kaspanet.io. And the only safe places to start a download are kaspa.org/hodl and the community wiki's wallet list — never a search result.

Deprecated, and still being recommended elsewhere

This is where outdated guides actively hurt people. Two wallets that appear in years-old "best of" lists are officially retired by their own developers:

  • KDX — its developer states it plainly: KDX is replaced by Kaspa NG, is no longer maintained, and will cease to function as the network upgrades. Do not start anything new on it.
  • The old web wallet (wallet.kaspanet.io) — marked "no longer maintained" in the community wiki, superseded by Kaspa NG. If your coins still sit there, they are not lost: your seed phrase is what owns the coins, not the app. Restore it in a maintained wallet and move on.

The general rule this teaches: a wallet recommendation has a shelf life. Check the project's last release date before trusting any list, including this one — this page was last verified on 7 August 2026 against the community wiki.

Why your address keeps changing

The question every Kaspa newcomer asks, usually in mild panic: "my wallet shows a different receive address than yesterday — did something break?"

No. Kaspa wallets are HD wallets on a UTXO chain, like Bitcoin. Your wallet generates a fresh receive address after use for privacy, so outside observers cannot link all your payments together. The two facts that matter:

  • Old addresses stay valid forever. The community wiki says it directly: the wallet changes the address when it detects use, "but it doesn't invalidate these addresses, they keep being absolutely functional." Coins sent to an address you handed out last year still arrive.
  • Your balance is the sum across all your addresses. One seed phrase controls the whole family of addresses. The number in your wallet is already the total.
One real caveat

Seed phrases are not guaranteed to be compatible between different Kaspa wallets — the wiki maintains a dedicated compatibility chart. Before you wipe wallet A after importing its seed into wallet B, confirm the full balance actually shows up in B.

The five rules that keep your coins

  1. Download only via the two official directories. kaspa.org/hodl or the wiki wallet list, then follow their links to the store or repository. Never install a wallet from a search result or a link someone sent you — sites impersonating an "official Kaspa wallet" exist right now, and a documented "claim your Kaspa" airdrop site was draining wallets as recently as 2025.
  2. Your seed phrase is never typed anywhere except a wallet you are restoring. Not into a website, not into a "validation" form, not to support staff. The official wiki puts one scam pattern beyond doubt: any offer asking you to send coins to an address "for a new version" is, in their words, a 100% scam attempt.
  3. Nobody legitimate contacts you first. Not admins, not support, not "the team". Anyone sliding into your DMs about your wallet is the scam, every time. This is written in pinned messages across the official Kaspa groups for a reason.
  4. Test with a small amount first. kaspa.org's own advice: send a small test transaction, verify it arrives, then move the rest. And check the first and last characters of every address before confirming — malware that swaps addresses in your clipboard is a real category.
  5. Match the wallet to the amount. Pocket money can live in a hot wallet. Anything that would hurt to lose belongs on hardware, where the keys never touch an internet-connected device.
