Kaspa web wallet

Kaspa NG runs in the browser as a web app at kaspa-ng.org, built from the same Rust code as the desktop version. The community wiki adds one warning that matters, in web mode the wallet keeps your keys in the browser's local storage. What that means in practice is on its own page, is a Kaspa web wallet safe?

KasVault is a web interface for a Ledger device. It talks to the Ledger over WebUSB, which its own documentation says works in Chrome and Edge, and the keys never leave the device.

Kasware is an open-source browser extension. The community wiki lists it as the wallet that shows KRC-20 tokens.

The old web wallet at wallet.kaspanet.io is no longer maintained, see deprecated wallets below.

Kaspa wallet for iOS

Kaspium is a non-custodial, open-source wallet for iOS and Android. Version 1.3.0 reached the App Store on 21 August 2026, the same day as the GitHub release.

Tangem uses an iPhone app together with its hardware card. App version 6.3.3 is listed in the App Store with 25 September 2026.

Kaspa wallet for Android

Kaspium is the same open-source wallet as on iOS, v1.3.0 on GitHub since 21 August 2026.

Tangem has an Android app for its card, v6.3.4 on GitHub since 25 September 2026.

Kaspa desktop wallet

Kaspa NG is the desktop wallet for Windows, macOS and Linux, v2.0.1 since 29 June 2026. The wiki calls it the successor of both KDX and the old web wallet, and it reads the older 12-word seeds those two created.

The command line wallet that ships with the Kaspa node software is for people who run their own node. The wiki has a setup guide.

Kaspa hardware wallet

Ledger keeps the Kaspa keys on the device. Kaspa on a Ledger runs only through KasVault, a third-party wallet, in Chrome or Edge. It does not run in Ledger Live. Ledger's own Kaspa page says to connect the device to a compatible third-party wallet. Earlier versions of this page said Kaspa ran inside Ledger Live; we corrected that on 6 October 2026 after reading Ledger's page.

OneKey supports Kaspa since May 2023 according to the community wiki, with the keys on the device and the OneKey app on the phone or computer.

Tangem supports Kaspa since April 2023 according to the community wiki. The key is created on the card and never leaves it, the app only shows balances and passes transactions to the card.

Trezor does not support Kaspa. Trezor's own page says so, "Currently, Trezor does not support Kaspa" (trezor.io, read on 6 October 2026).

Deprecated, and still being recommended elsewhere

This is where outdated guides actively hurt people. Two wallets that appear in years-old "best of" lists are officially retired by their own developers:

  • KDX — its developer states it plainly: KDX is replaced by Kaspa NG, is no longer maintained, and will cease to function as the network upgrades. Do not start anything new on it.
  • The old web wallet (wallet.kaspanet.io) — marked "no longer maintained" in the community wiki, superseded by Kaspa NG. If your coins still sit there, they are not lost: your seed phrase is what owns the coins, not the app. Restore it in a maintained wallet and move on.

The general rule this teaches: a wallet recommendation has a shelf life. Check the project's last release date before trusting any list, including this one — this page was last checked on 6 October 2026, see the date under the heading.

Why your address keeps changing

The question every Kaspa newcomer asks, usually in mild panic: "my wallet shows a different receive address than yesterday — did something break?"

No. Kaspa wallets are HD wallets on a UTXO chain, like Bitcoin. Your wallet generates a fresh receive address after use for privacy, so outside observers cannot link all your payments together. The two facts that matter:

  • Old addresses stay valid forever. The community wiki says it directly: the wallet changes the address when it detects use, "but it doesn't invalidate these addresses, they keep being absolutely functional." Coins sent to an address you handed out last year still arrive.
  • Your balance is the sum across all your addresses. One seed phrase controls the whole family of addresses. The number in your wallet is already the total.

To track your portfolio and tokens, see kashub.fyi.

One real caveat

Seed phrases are not guaranteed to be compatible between different Kaspa wallets — the wiki maintains a dedicated compatibility chart. Before you wipe wallet A after importing its seed into wallet B, confirm the full balance actually shows up in B.

The five rules that keep your coins

  1. Download only via the two official directories. kaspa.org/hodl or the wiki wallet list, then follow their links to the store or repository. Never install a wallet from a search result or a link someone sent you — sites impersonating an "official Kaspa wallet" exist right now, and a documented "claim your Kaspa" airdrop site was draining wallets as recently as 2025.
  2. Your seed phrase is never typed anywhere except a wallet you are restoring. Not into a website, not into a "validation" form, not to support staff. The official wiki puts one scam pattern beyond doubt: any offer asking you to send coins to an address "for a new version" is, in their words, a 100% scam attempt.
  3. Nobody legitimate contacts you first. Not admins, not support, not "the team". Anyone sliding into your DMs about your wallet is the scam, every time. This is written in pinned messages across the official Kaspa groups for a reason.
  4. Test with a small amount first. kaspa.org's own advice: send a small test transaction, verify it arrives, then move the rest. And check the first and last characters of every address before confirming — malware that swaps addresses in your clipboard is a real category.
  5. Match the wallet to the amount. Pocket money can live in a hot wallet. Anything that would hurt to lose belongs on hardware, where the keys never touch an internet-connected device.

Questions people ask

Is a Kaspa web wallet safe?

It can be, for small amounts. In web mode Kaspa NG keeps the keys in your browser's local storage, so anything that can read that browser can reach them. For larger amounts a hardware wallet keeps the keys off the computer. The details are on is a Kaspa web wallet safe?

Which hardware wallets support Kaspa?

Ledger through KasVault, OneKey and Tangem, checked on 6 October 2026. The community wiki also lists Cool Wallet and the SafePal X1.

Does Trezor support Kaspa?

No. Trezor's own page says "Currently, Trezor does not support Kaspa", read on 6 October 2026.

Why does my Kaspa address keep changing?

Your wallet shows a fresh receive address after each use, for privacy, and the old ones keep working. The full explanation is in why your address keeps changing.
